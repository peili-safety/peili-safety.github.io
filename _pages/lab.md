We investigate the scientific foundations of AI for transportation: how to learn from heterogeneous and incomplete observations, incorporate physical and behavioral knowledge into models, and reason reliably about dynamic traffic environments. Our research develops spatiotemporal models, multimodal and generative learning methods, and transportation digital twins, supported by benchmarks and empirical evaluation. We study representation transfer, uncertainty, and the limits of inference from available evidence, connecting methodological advances to transportation safety, operations, and connected and automated mobility.

### Research Support

- **Lightweight Vision-Language Models for Weather Condition Understanding at the Edge (2026–2027)** — Supported by the Wyoming NASA EPSCoR Faculty Research Award. The project aims to distill large vision-language models into lightweight models that interpret precipitation, visibility, and road surface conditions from roadside camera imagery and provide natural-language explanations. Planned outputs include an open weather visual question-answering dataset, compact models, and an interactive interface evaluated on edge computing platforms.

- **SCOUT-WY: AI-Enabled Sparse-Camera Observation and Unified Tracking for Rural Wyoming Corridors (2026-2027)** — Supported by the University of Wyoming College of Engineering and Physical Sciences (CEPS) through the Engineering Initiative seed grant program. The project aims to link vehicle observations across sparse roadside cameras using vehicle re-identification and transportation-aware spatiotemporal reasoning, supporting travel-time estimation, traffic monitoring, and anomaly detection.

- **Wyoming Center for Artificial Intelligence in Transportation (WyCAIT) (2026-2027)** — Supported by the University of Wyoming Science Institute. The proposed center focuses on multimodal sensing, resilient AI for sparse data and limited connectivity, and corridor-scale digital twins and human–AI systems for rural transportation safety, operations, and workforce development.

- **Wisconsin Highway Delay Causation Study (2025–2026)** — Supported by the Wisconsin Department of Transportation (WisDOT). The study developed a framework integrating travel-time data, traffic volumes, crash and incident records, lane closures, and weather to measure recurring and nonrecurring highway delay and examine associated contributing factors at statewide and corridor scales. [Final report](https://wisconsindot.gov/documents2/research/0092-25-22-final-report.pdf).
